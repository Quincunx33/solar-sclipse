# Historical eclipse research

## Sources
1. NASA, Solar Eclipses of Historical Interest: https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/SEhistory/SEhistory.html
2. NASA, Solar Eclipses catalog and 2021–2030 table: https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/solar.html
3. timeanddate, Solar Eclipses in History: https://www.timeanddate.com/eclipse/solar-eclipse-history.html

## Verified archive candidates
- 1851-07-28 — Total — first photograph of the Sun's corona, per timeanddate; NASA historical catalog includes the event.
- 1868-08-18 — Total — helium evidence discovered during the eclipse, per timeanddate; NASA historical catalog includes the event.
- 1919-05-29 — Total — Eddington eclipse used to test gravitational deflection / general relativity, per timeanddate; NASA historical catalog includes the event.
- 1973-06-30 — Total — listed in NASA historical catalog; notable for unusually long totality and scientific observation context.
- 1991-07-11 — Total — NASA historical catalog; major total eclipse across Hawaii, Mexico, Central America, and South America.
- 1999-08-11 — Total — NASA lists a dedicated event page; widely known European total eclipse.
- 2009-07-22 — Total — longest total solar eclipse of the 21st century at 6m39s, per timeanddate; NASA lists it as a dedicated event.
- 2017-08-21 — Total — NASA historical catalog; Great American Eclipse across the United States.
- 2024-04-08 — Total — NASA 2021–2030 table lists 4m28s central duration and totality across Mexico, central United States, and eastern Canada.
- 2026-08-12 — Total — NASA 2021–2030 table lists 2m18s central duration and totality across the Arctic, Greenland, Iceland, and Spain.

## Notes for UI copy
NASA warns that historical associations may be uncertain or apocryphal; archive cards should distinguish documented scientific milestones from cultural or historical associations. The initial implementation should use a curated list with year, date, type, maximum duration, visibility region, significance, and source URL.
